BEFORE DAY ONE · BIBLICAL TYPOLOGY

Learning to Read the Bible Through Types & Shadows

When you study the Word, learn to read each passage in its own setting and as part of the Bible’s one great story. The beginning helps explain the end, and the end often sends you back to the beginning with clearer eyes. Scripture itself teaches us to notice these God-given patterns. The study method is commonly called biblical typology—recognizing real people, events, institutions, sacrifices, offices, and patterns in earlier Scripture that God later fulfills or brings into fuller light, above all in Jesus Christ.

Begin with Jesus’ own method:
Read Luke 24:25–27 and Luke 24:44–47. After His resurrection, Jesus went back through Moses, the Prophets, and the Psalms and showed how the Scriptures pointed to Him. Also read John 5:39, 46.

The Bible itself gives us the vocabulary

TYPE / PATTERNRomans 5:14 calls Adam a “type” of the One to come. 1 Corinthians 10:6, 11 says Israel’s wilderness events happened as examples/patterns for us.
SHADOWColossians 2:16–17 says certain Old Covenant observances were a shadow, while the substance belongs to Christ. Hebrews 10:1 calls the law a shadow of the good things to come.
COPY / HEAVENLY REALITYHebrews 8:5 calls the tabernacle ministry a copy and shadow of heavenly things. Hebrews 9:23–24 contrasts earthly copies with the heavenly reality into which Christ entered.
FULFILLMENT IN CHRISTThe goal is not clever symbolism. The goal is to see how God’s earlier patterns prepare us to understand the person and work of Jesus.

The beginning reflects the end—and the end helps explain the beginning

Genesis introduces realities that Revelation deliberately brings to completion. This does not mean every detail is a secret code. It means the Bible has a unified storyline, vocabulary, imagery, promises, and patterns.

The Tree of LifeGenesis 2:9 introduces the tree of life. It reappears in the restored creation in Revelation 22:2, 14.
The Serpent and His DefeatGenesis 3:15 announces conflict and promised victory. Revelation 12:9 identifies the ancient serpent, and Revelation 20:10 shows his final defeat.
The Curse RemovedThe ground is cursed in Genesis 3:17. In the final restoration, Revelation 22:3 says there will no longer be any curse.
God Dwelling With HumanityFrom Eden, through tabernacle and temple, the story moves toward Revelation 21:3: God dwelling with His people.
The RiverA river waters Eden in Genesis 2:10. A river of the water of life flows from God’s throne in Revelation 22:1–2.
Bride and CovenantGenesis 2:21–24 establishes marriage; Ephesians 5:31–32 says that mystery speaks of Christ and the church; Revelation closes with bridal imagery in Revelation 19:7–9; 21:2, 9.

What exactly is a type?

A type is something real in the earlier biblical story that God uses to establish a pattern which is later fulfilled more fully. The earlier event is not imaginary; it mattered in its own time. But God also built it into a larger story.

A shadow gives a true outline without being the final substance. When the substance arrives, the shadow makes more sense. Colossians 2:17 says the substance is Christ. So the safest typology moves from the text, through Scripture’s own connections, toward Christ—not from our imagination back into the text.

Important guardrail: Do not turn the Bible into a puzzle where every color, number, tent peg, or incidental detail must secretly mean something. Start with connections Scripture itself makes. Preserve the original historical meaning. Let clearer passages control less-clear patterns. A possible resemblance is not the same thing as a biblical type.

Example 1 — The Passover Lamb

This is one of the clearest ways to learn the method because the New Testament itself identifies Christ with Passover.

Earlier Scripture
Fulfillment / Explanation
Exodus 12:3–6
A lamb is selected; it must be without blemish.
John 1:29 presents Jesus as the Lamb of God. 1 Peter 1:18–19 speaks of Christ as a lamb without blemish or spot.
Exodus 12:7, 13
The blood marks the house, and judgment passes over where the blood is seen.
1 Corinthians 5:7 states the connection directly: Christ, our Passover, has been sacrificed.
Exodus 12:46
No bone of the Passover lamb is to be broken.
John 19:33–36 records that Jesus’ bones were not broken and explicitly connects the event to Scripture.

How to think: first understand Exodus 12 as Israel’s real deliverance from Egypt. Then follow the New Testament’s own trail to Christ. The historical event remains true, and at the same time God has made it part of a larger redemptive pattern.

Example 2 — The Bronze Serpent

Read Numbers 21:4–9, then immediately read John 3:14–15. Here Jesus gives the interpretation Himself: as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so the Son of Man must be lifted up. That “as … so” structure is typology made explicit by Christ.

Numbers 21:4–9→John 3:14–15→Christ lifted up

Example 3 — Manna and the Bread of Life

Read Exodus 16 and then John 6:31–35, 48–51. Israel truly received bread in the wilderness. Jesus then teaches that the Father gives the true bread from heaven and identifies Himself as the Bread of Life. The earlier provision prepares the language by which Jesus reveals Himself.

Exodus 16→John 6→Jesus, the Bread of Life

More types Scripture itself identifies

Adam → ChristRomans 5:14–19; 1 Corinthians 15:45–49. Adam is explicitly called a type of the One to come; Christ is the last Adam.
Jonah → Death & ResurrectionJonah 1:17; Matthew 12:39–40. Jesus Himself compares Jonah’s three days with the Son of Man’s burial.
Melchizedek → Christ’s PriesthoodGenesis 14:18–20; Psalm 110:4; Hebrews 7. Hebrews develops the earlier priest-king into an extended explanation of Christ’s priesthood.
Tabernacle / Priesthood → Heavenly MinistryExodus 25–30; Hebrews 8:1–5; 9:11–24. Hebrews calls the earthly sanctuary a copy and shadow and presents Christ as the greater High Priest.
Wilderness Events → Instruction for UsExodus–Numbers; 1 Corinthians 10:1–11. Paul says those events became examples/patterns and warnings for later believers.
Marriage → Christ and the ChurchGenesis 2:24; Ephesians 5:31–32. Paul quotes Genesis and says the mystery refers to Christ and the church.

How to study this way without getting lost

  1. 01
    Read the text in context.
    What actually happened? Who is speaking? What did it mean in that moment?
  2. 02
    Notice repeated biblical patterns.
    Sacrifice, covenant, exodus, wilderness, priesthood, temple, king, bride, son, lamb, bread, water, rest, resurrection.
  3. 03
    Search for Scripture’s own cross-references.
    Does Jesus, Paul, Hebrews, Peter, or Revelation return to this person, event, object, or phrase?
  4. 04
    Ask how Christ brings the pattern to fullness.
    Do not force Christ into the passage; follow the road Scripture itself builds toward Him.
  5. 05
    Then ask what changes in you.
    Typology is not merely intellectual discovery. Seeing Christ more clearly should deepen faith, worship, obedience, humility, and hope.
A sentence to carry through all 100 days:
Read what is there. Notice what repeats. Follow where Scripture takes it. Look for Christ. Then obey what you have seen.

Four questions for every day

  1. 01What does this passage teach me about God?
  2. 02What does it reveal about the human heart?
  3. 03Is there a type, shadow, promise, covenant, repeated image, or pattern that later Scripture develops or fulfills in Christ?
  4. 04What must change in me because I have read it?

Do not rush. You are not trying to hunt for hidden codes. You are learning the vocabulary of Scripture so that Genesis can help you understand Revelation, Hebrews can illuminate Leviticus, John can send you back to Exodus and Numbers, and the whole Bible can increasingly lead your eyes to Christ.
