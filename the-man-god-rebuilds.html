Before Day One

Learning to See the Bible

The Bible is one unfolding story of creation, fall, redemption, restoration, and the reign of God, with Jesus Christ at its center.

What are types and shadows?

Scripture sometimes uses real people, events, sacrifices, offices, and institutions in the Old Testament to prepare us to understand Christ and His work. Hebrews 10:1 speaks of the law as a shadow of good things to come, and Colossians 2:17 describes certain observances as shadows whose substance belongs to Christ.

Adam helps us understand Christ as the last Adam. The Passover lamb prepares us to see Christ our Passover. Manna prepares us for the Bread of Life. The high priest prepares us for Jesus our perfect High Priest. David's throne prepares us for the eternal King.

A guardrail: not every detail is secretly symbolic. Let Scripture interpret Scripture. When the New Testament clearly identifies a type, stand firmly on it. Elsewhere, recognize biblical patterns humbly without forcing meanings into the text.

Four questions for every day

1. What does this teach me about God?

2. What does it reveal about the human heart?

3. How does it connect to Christ and God's redemptive plan?

4. What must change in me because I have read it?

Do not rush. You are not trying to finish the Bible. You are learning to walk with its Author.
